---
title: "The scalar glueball on center-vortex-removed SU(2) lattice ensembles: a data note"
author: "Turaq Rayn-McLean"
date: "October 30, 2026 (draft, not submitted)"
abstract: |
  We measure the zero-momentum $0^{++}$ glueball correlator on SU(2) Wilson-action ensembles at $\beta = 2.3$, $2.4$ and $2.5$ in three readings of the same configurations: the original links, the center-projected $Z_2$ links in direct maximal center gauge, and the vortex-removed links of de Forcrand and D'Elia. A literature search found no published glueball measurement on vortex-removed ensembles; this is new as far as we found, not proven new. At $\beta = 2.4$ the result is clean: the vortex-removed string tension is zero within errors, and the vortex-removed $0^{++}$ correlator is a single exponential with mass $2.40(8)\sqrt{\sigma}$ (original string tension), $0.71(4)$ of the original glueball mass, unchanged between $12^4$ and $16^4$ (ratio $0.98(4)$) and below the free two-gluon level. At $\beta = 2.5$ the vortex-removed mass is also volume independent ($20^4/16^4$ ratio $0.94(6)$), but our pre-registered premise check fails there: a short-time string-tension estimator leaves about $0.21$ of the original string tension on the vortex-removed ensemble, while longer-time estimators are consistent with zero. A paired study with the best of 16 gauge copies does not remove that residual ($0.225(10)$), lowers the center-projected string tension to $0.73$ of the full one, and shifts the vortex-removed mass by $2.3\sigma$. At $\beta = 2.3$ the vortex-removed effective mass does not plateau on the larger box. Applying our pre-registered rules exactly, the combined outcome is inconclusive. We publish the measurements, the failure modes, data and code.
---

# 1. Limitations, stated first

- Monte Carlo evidence on small lattices: boxes from $8^4$ to $20^4$, at most two volumes per coupling in each volume test.
- SU(2) only, Wilson action, three couplings ($\beta = 2.3, 2.4, 2.5$), no continuum extrapolation. Nothing here bears on the continuum mass-gap problem.
- The vortex-removed (VR) ensemble is gauge dependent: it is defined through direct maximal center gauge (MCG), which has many local maxima (Gribov copies). The main runs use one gauge copy per configuration; the $\beta = 2.5$ follow-up shows that the VR mass and the center-projected (CP) string tension move with the copy.
- VR configurations are not generated by a local reflection-positive action, so VR correlators need not be positive sums of exponentials; "mass" means the decay rate read by our estimator.
- Estimator caveats: effective masses are resolved only to $t \approx 3$; the volume tests use a fixed plateau rule on $m(1), m(2)$; the string-tension premise check uses $V(R) = \ln W(R,3)/W(R,4)$, which at $\beta = 2.5$ probes short physical times. Our $\beta = 2.5$ unit $a\sqrt{\sigma} = 0.2072(10)$ is about 10% above the literature value with this estimator. Original glueball masses come out about 10% below continuum values [9, 10].
- Not blind: the original-ensemble glueball mass was known in advance; the first series' estimators were chosen after seeing exploratory $8^4$ and $12^4$ data; one deviation (GEVP $t_0$) was made after testing on already-seen data; the long-time estimators of sec. 4.3 and the gauge-copy method of sec. 4.4 were chosen after the $\beta = 2.5$ premise failure was known. Each series did fix its rules and wall-clock stopping time before its own production data existed.

# 2. Background: what is known and what is new

De Forcrand and D'Elia [1] removed center vortices from SU(2) configurations by multiplying each link by its center projection in direct MCG, and found that confinement is lost (Creutz ratios tend to zero, large-$T$ slopes do not grow with $R$) at $\beta = 2.4$ on $16^4$. Pepe and de Forcrand [2] found the same at $\beta = 2.3$ to $2.5$ with Laplacian center gauge. Direct MCG and center dominance go back to Del Debbio et al. [3, 4]. Vortex removal has since been used for gluon and ghost propagators [5, 6, 7] and for the light hadron spectrum and chiral symmetry in SU(3) [8], where it restores chiral symmetry. Glueballs after Abelian projection were measured in [11, 12]. Langfeld and Schafke [13] measured $0^+$ and $2^+$ screening masses on thick-vortex ensembles obtained by gauge-invariant cooling, which is a different reduction from the one used here. The dependence of center-projected observables on the MCG gauge copy is documented in [14, 15]: better maxima lower the projected string tension, and the effect weakens on larger volumes.

New here, as far as our search found (arXiv, INSPIRE including the papers citing [1], 1999 to October 2026): (a) the $0^{++}$ glueball correlator on VR ensembles and on thin $Z_2$ CP ensembles, with volume tests; (b) the observation that at $\beta = 2.5$ a short-time estimator leaves a residual VR string tension of about 0.2 while long-time estimators do not; (c) a paired gauge-copy study of that residual and of the VR mass. Item (b) is consistent with [1, 2], which used long-distance observables.

# 3. Method

**Ensembles.** SU(2) Wilson action, heat bath with four over-relaxation steps per compound sweep, hot start, 300 thermalization sweeps. Three series, each with independent streams stopped by a wall clock fixed before any of its data were analysed:

- Series 1: $\beta = 2.3$ on $8^4$, $12^4$, $16^4$ and $\beta = 2.4$ on $12^4$, $16^4$, $T = L$, a measurement every 2 sweeps (the $8^4$ and part of the $12^4$ statistics come from earlier exploratory streams with the same action and gauge fixing).
- Series 2: $\beta = 2.3$ on $12^3\times 32$ and $16^3 \times 32$; $\beta = 2.5$ on $16^4$ and $20^4$; a measurement every 2 sweeps.
- Series 3 (gauge-copy study): $\beta = 2.5$ on $16^4$ (8 streams) and $20^4$ (2 streams), $\beta = 2.3$ on $12^3 \times 32$ (2 streams, control); a measurement every 10 sweeps.

**Center projection and vortex removal.** Direct MCG maximizes $\sum_{x,\mu} |\mathrm{tr}\,U_\mu(x)|^2$ by local exact maximization with over-relaxation ($\omega = 1.85$) until the relative change is below $10^{-8}$. With $Z_\mu(x) = \mathrm{sign}\,\mathrm{tr}\,U_\mu(x)$, the CP links are $Z_\mu(x)$ and the VR links are $Z_\mu(x) U_\mu(x)$ [1]. On every plaquette of every configuration we assert $P_{\mathrm{orig}} = Z_P P_{\mathrm{VR}}$, which fails if the gauge fixing is not a gauge transformation. Series 1 and 2 use one gauge copy (over-relaxation started from the configuration as generated). Series 3 fixes every configuration twice: the same single copy ("single"), and the best functional among 16 candidates, the single copy plus 15 Haar-random gauge transforms each relaxed to convergence ("best of 16"). Both are measured on the same configuration, so differences are paired.

**Glueball.** Zero-momentum sums of spatial Wilson loops ($1\times1$, $2\times2$, $3\times3$, $4\times4$, $1\times2$, $2\times3$, $3\times4$) at three APE smearing levels ($\alpha = 0.5$; $(0,2,8)$ steps at $\beta = 2.3$, $(0,4,15)$ at $2.4$, $(0,8,31)$ at $2.5$, scaled with $(a\sqrt{\sigma})^{-2}$): basis A, 21 operators (CP: the 7 shapes unsmeared). Vacuum-subtracted correlators averaged over all time slices, normalized by $\sqrt{\mathrm{diag}\,C(0)}$, ground vector from the GEVP at $(t_0, t) = (0, 1)$, effective masses $m(t) = \ln C(t)/C(t+1)$. Plateau rule, fixed in advance: if $m(2)$ has relative error below 30% and $|m(1) - m(2)| < 1.5\sigma$ (jackknife of the difference), quote $m(1)$ ("plateau"); if $m(2)$ is that precise but differs, quote $m(2)$ ("late"); otherwise quote $m(1)$ as an "upper bound"; a quoted value with relative error above 30% is "noisy".

**String tension.** Estimator E1: $V(R) = \ln W(R,3)/W(R,4)$, fitted with $V_0 + \sigma R - e/R$, $e$ free, $R = 2,\dots,L/2$, with spatially smeared Wilson loops. Series 3 adds E1$'$ ($R \ge 4$), E2 ($T = 5 \to 6$ and $6 \to 7$) and Creutz ratios $\chi(R,R)$. Ratios $\sigma_X/\sigma_{\mathrm{ORIG}}$ use the same estimator on the same configurations.

**Errors.** Delete-one-block jackknife, block length at least twice the largest integrated autocorrelation time (Madras-Sokal window, $c = 6$), 8 to 20 blocks per stream; a bin-size scan is reported in the data. Thermalization check: first quarter dropped if its plaquette differs from the rest by more than $3\sigma$ (one stream in series 1).

**Pre-registered tests.** Premise C0: $\sigma_{\mathrm{VR}}/\sigma_{\mathrm{ORIG}} < 0.15$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{CP}}/\sigma_{\mathrm{ORIG}} \in [0.6, 1.4]$ with E1. Volume test at each coupling: $\rho = m(L_{\mathrm{large}})/m(L_{\mathrm{small}})$ in lattice units; "gap" if $\rho$ is within $2\sigma$ of 1 and more than $2\sigma$ from the box ratio $L_{\mathrm{small}}/L_{\mathrm{large}}$, "gapless" the reverse, otherwise inconclusive; interpreted only if the original-ensemble control reads "gap".

# 4. Results

## 4.1 Statistics and checks

Plaquettes: $0.60228$ ($\beta = 2.3$), $0.63001$ ($2.4$), $0.6519$ to $0.6520$ ($2.5$), all within $3\times10^{-4}$ of the literature values. Integrated autocorrelation times are 0.5 to 1.6 measurements (one short stream 3.0). No assertion fired in any stream. MCG functional: $0.753$ ($2.3$), $0.773$ ($2.4$), $0.791$ ($2.5$, single copy). P-vortex density: $0.090$, $0.055$, $0.032$.

# 5. Pre-registered outcomes

Each series fixed its combined rule in advance; we report them as written.

- Series 1: "gapless", triggered by $\beta = 2.3$ alone ($\rho_{\mathrm{VR}} = 0.733(111)$) while $\beta = 2.4$ was a clean "gap".
- Series 2: the combined rule as written reads "gap survives vortex removal (in this window)" ($\beta = 2.5$ gap, $2.4$ gap, no significant drop of $m_{\mathrm{VR}}/\sqrt\sigma$ from $2.4$ to $2.5$, $2.3$ not gapless). The rule did not say whether the series 1 consequence of a C0 failure (that coupling does not enter the verdict) carries over; with it, the outcome is inconclusive.
- Series 3 was designed to settle that by test. Its rule: if the best-of-16 VR E1 ratio is still $\ge 0.15$, the combined outcome is inconclusive, because the $\beta = 2.5$ VR point is not a clean vortex-free measurement under C0. The ratio is $0.225(10)$, so the **combined outcome is inconclusive**. Of the pre-registered diagnoses, a gauge-fixing artifact is excluded (the residual does not fall with better copies); "measurement artifact of the short-time estimator" and "real residual" are not separated, because the long-range estimators at $16^4$ have errors of about $0.2$.

# 6. Discussion

The firm result is at $\beta = 2.4$: on an ensemble whose string tension is zero within errors, the vortex-removed $0^{++}$ channel has a volume-independent decay rate of about $0.7$ of the original glueball mass, below the free two-gluon level of both boxes. At $\beta = 2.5$ the same picture holds for the mass, and the long-time string-tension estimators agree with [1, 2] that vortex removal removes the string tension, but the pre-registered short-time check does not pass, and the VR mass moves at the $2\sigma$ level with the gauge copy. The center-projected string tension depends strongly on the gauge copy ($0.99$ of the full one with a single copy, $0.73$ with the best of 16, falling monotonically with the functional, as in [14, 15]); since the VR links are the original links with the center part divided out, the VR ensemble itself depends on the copy, and a VR mass should be quoted with that systematic. At $\beta = 2.3$ the VR correlator is not a clean exponential at the available times.

What would settle it: a premise check built on long-time or Creutz-ratio estimators fixed before new data, larger $T$ at $\beta = 2.5$ and finer couplings, a third box per coupling, and a systematic gauge-copy (or Laplacian center gauge) study of the VR mass.
