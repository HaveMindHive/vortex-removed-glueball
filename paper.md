title: "The scalar glueball on center-vortex-removed SU(2) lattice ensembles: a data note"
author: "Turaq Rayn-McLean"
date: "October 30, 2026 (draft, revised after referee review, not submitted)"
abstract: |
  We measure the zero-momentum $0^{++}$ glueball correlator on SU(2) Wilson-action ensembles at $\beta = 2.3$, $2.4$ and $2.5$ in three readings of the same configurations: the original links, the center-projected $Z_2$ links in direct maximal center gauge, and the vortex-removed links of de Forcrand and D'Elia. A literature search found no published glueball measurement on vortex-removed ensembles; this is new as far as we found, not proven new. The cleanest point in this data set is $\beta = 2.4$, with the caveat that only two couplings enter any vortex-removed mass comparison and there is no continuum statement. There, the vortex-removed string tension from our short-time estimator is zero within errors; a reanalysis of the stored Wilson loops done after referee review finds the long-time estimators consistent with zero only at the largest distances we resolve (Creutz ratios at $R = 6, 7$), with a residual rise at intermediate distances ($\chi(5,5)$ is $0.12(2)$ of the original). The vortex-removed $0^{++}$ effective masses are consistent with a constant over $t = 1$ to $3$, with mass $2.40(8)\sqrt{\sigma}$ in our own string-tension unit ($2.52(8)\sqrt{\sigma}$ in the literature unit), $0.71(4)$ of the original glueball mass, and unchanged between $12^4$ and $16^4$ (ratio $0.98(4)$, $5.6\sigma$ from the box ratio $0.75$). The $\beta = 2.4$ vortex-removed mass is from one gauge copy and carries an untested gauge-copy systematic; at the neighbouring couplings the best of 16 copies moved the mass by about 20%. At $\beta = 2.5$ the vortex-removed mass is also volume independent ($20^4/16^4$ ratio $0.94(6)$), but our pre-registered premise check fails there: the short-time estimator leaves about $0.21$ of the original string tension on the vortex-removed ensemble, and so do E1$'$ ($R \ge 4$) and $\chi(6,6)$; E2 at $T = 5, 6$ is at or below zero and $\chi(7,7)$ is consistent with zero. With the best of 16 gauge copies the residual does not fall ($0.225(10)$), the center-projected string tension drops to $0.73$ of the full one, and the vortex-removed mass shifts by $2.3\sigma$. At $\beta = 2.3$ the vortex-removed effective mass does not plateau on the larger box. Applying our pre-registered rules exactly, the combined outcome is inconclusive. We publish the measurements, the failure modes, data and code.

# 1. Limitations, stated first

- Monte Carlo evidence on small lattices: boxes from $8^4$ to $20^4$, at most two volumes per coupling in each volume test.
- SU(2) only, Wilson action, three couplings ($\beta = 2.3, 2.4, 2.5$), no continuum extrapolation. Only $2.4$ and $2.5$ enter any vortex-removed mass comparison, because the $\beta = 2.3$ channel is not under control (sec. 4.2). Nothing here bears on the continuum mass-gap problem.
- The vortex-removed (VR) ensemble is gauge dependent: it is defined through direct maximal center gauge (MCG), which has many local maxima (Gribov copies). The main runs use one gauge copy per configuration. The gauge-copy study (sec. 4.4) was done at $\beta = 2.5$ and at a $\beta = 2.3$ control, not at $\beta = 2.4$; there the VR mass moved with the copy by $+0.109(48)$ and $-0.192(109)$ respectively, about 20% of the mass. Copies closer to the global maximum (simulated annealing, many copies [14]) and Laplacian center gauge [2] were not tested; the VR mass may depend on that choice.
- VR configurations are not generated by a local reflection-positive action, so VR correlators need not be positive sums of exponentials; "mass" means the decay rate read by our estimator.
- Removing the vortices flips the sign of the trace on every P-plaquette, so the VR plaquette is much lower than the original ($0.585$ vs $0.630$ at $\beta = 2.4$, $0.623$ vs $0.652$ at $2.5$). VR configurations therefore carry a density of lattice-scale defects, and the VR $0^{++}$ channel could be partly a defect (ultraviolet) effect. The only check here is that $m_{\mathrm{VR}}/\sqrt{\sigma}$ changes little between two couplings ($2.52 \to 2.38$ in literature units).
- Estimator caveats: effective masses are resolved only to $t \approx 3$; the volume tests use a fixed plateau rule on $m(1), m(2)$; the pre-registered string-tension premise check uses $V(R) = \ln W(R,3)/W(R,4)$, which probes short physical times and, with a free Coulomb term, can absorb part of a potential that rises and then flattens inside the fit window (sec. 4.3). With this estimator our units $a\sqrt{\sigma}$ differ from the literature values [9, 29] by factors $1.04$ ($\beta = 2.3$, $16^4$), $0.95$ ($2.3$, $16^3\times32$), $1.05$ ($2.4$) and $1.10$ ($2.5$); we give masses in both units. In literature units the original glueball masses are $1$ to $6\%$ below the continuum value $3.78(7)\sqrt{\sigma}$ [10].
- Polyakov loops were not measured on the VR ensembles, so we cannot say whether center symmetry is broken there at $T = L$, which bears on which finite-volume states the $0^{++}$ operators can couple to (sec. 6).
- Not blind: the original-ensemble glueball mass was known in advance; the series 1 estimators were chosen after seeing exploratory $8^4$ and $12^4$ data, and those exploratory data are part of the series 1 sample (sec. 3); one deviation (GEVP $t_0$) was made after testing on already-seen data; the long-time estimators of sec. 4.3 and the gauge-copy method of sec. 4.4 were chosen after the $\beta = 2.5$ premise failure was known; the $\beta = 2.4$ long-time reanalysis was done after referee review. The rules of each series were written and committed to a private git repository before that series' new production data existed (internally time-stamped, not externally registered).

# 2. Background: what is known and what is new

Center vortices as the confining configurations are reviewed in [17]. De Forcrand and D'Elia [1] removed center vortices from SU(2) configurations by multiplying each link by its center projection in direct MCG, and found that confinement is lost (Creutz ratios tend to zero, large-$T$ slopes do not grow with $R$) at $\beta = 2.4$ on $16^4$. Pepe and de Forcrand [2] found the same at $\beta = 2.3$ to $2.5$ with Laplacian center gauge. Direct MCG and center dominance go back to Del Debbio et al. [3, 4]. Vortex removal has since been used for gluon and ghost propagators [5, 6, 7], the SU(2) quark propagator [30], the SU(3) string tension [31], and the light hadron spectrum and chiral symmetry in SU(3) [8], where it restores chiral symmetry. Biddle, Kamleh and Leinweber [16] found that the vortex-removed static potential is not described by $V_0 + \sigma R - e/R$ at long range and fitted it with a modified Coulomb term. Glueballs after Abelian projection were measured in [11, 12]. Langfeld and Schafke [13] measured $0^+$ and $2^+$ screening masses on thick-vortex ensembles obtained by gauge-invariant cooling, which is a different reduction from the one used here. The dependence of center-projected observables on the MCG gauge copy is documented in [14, 15]: better maxima lower the projected string tension, and the effect weakens on larger volumes.

New here, as far as our search found (arXiv, INSPIRE including the papers citing [1], 1999 to October 2026): (a) the $0^{++}$ glueball correlator on VR ensembles and on thin $Z_2$ CP ensembles, with volume tests; (b) a quantification of how a short-time string-tension estimator misreads the VR potential (a residual of about $0.2$ at $\beta = 2.5$ that long-time estimators do not show); the phenomenon itself is expected from [1, 2], which used long-distance observables, and from the failure of the standard potential form reported in [16]; (c) a paired gauge-copy study of that residual and of the VR mass.

# 3. Method

**Ensembles.** SU(2) Wilson action, heat bath [20, 21] with four over-relaxation [22, 23] steps per compound sweep, hot start, 300 thermalization sweeps. Three series, each with independent streams stopped by a wall clock fixed before any of its data were analysed:

- Series 1: $\beta = 2.3$ on $8^4$, $12^4$, $16^4$ and $\beta = 2.4$ on $12^4$, $16^4$, $T = L$, a measurement every 2 sweeps. Part of this sample existed before the series 1 rules were written: the $\beta = 2.3$ $8^4$ data (5000 measurements) and two of the three $12^4$ streams at each coupling (1700 of 3670 measurements at $\beta = 2.3$, 1750 of 3730 at $\beta = 2.4$) come from earlier exploratory streams with the same action and gauge fixing, which had been analysed. New after the rules: one $12^4$ stream per coupling and all $16^4$ streams (five at $2.3$, four at $2.4$; three of them were added about 2.4 h after launch with the same deadline, before any data were analysed).
- Series 2: $\beta = 2.3$ on $12^3\times 32$ and $16^3 \times 32$; $\beta = 2.5$ on $16^4$ and $20^4$; a measurement every 2 sweeps. All data new after the rules.
- Series 3 (gauge-copy study): $\beta = 2.5$ on $16^4$ (8 streams) and $20^4$ (2 streams), $\beta = 2.3$ on $12^3 \times 32$ (2 streams, control); a measurement every 10 sweeps. All data new after the rules.

**Center projection and vortex removal.** Direct MCG maximizes $\sum_{x,\mu} |\mathrm{tr}\,U_\mu(x)|^2$ by local exact maximization with over-relaxation ($\omega = 1.85$) until the relative change is below $10^{-8}$. With $Z_\mu(x) = \mathrm{sign}\,\mathrm{tr}\,U_\mu(x)$, the CP links are $Z_\mu(x)$ and the VR links are $Z_\mu(x) U_\mu(x)$ [1]. On every plaquette of every configuration we assert $P_{\mathrm{orig}} = Z_P P_{\mathrm{VR}}$, which fails if the gauge fixing is not a gauge transformation. Series 1 and 2 use one gauge copy (over-relaxation started from the configuration as generated). Series 3 fixes every configuration twice: the same single copy ("single"), and the best functional among 16 candidates, the single copy plus 15 Haar-random gauge transforms each relaxed to convergence ("best of 16"). Both are measured on the same configuration, so differences are paired.

**Glueball.** Zero-momentum sums of spatial Wilson loops [28] ($1\times1$, $2\times2$, $3\times3$, $4\times4$, $1\times2$, $2\times3$, $3\times4$) at three APE smearing levels [24] ($\alpha = 0.5$; $(0,2,8)$ steps at $\beta = 2.3$, $(0,4,15)$ at $2.4$, $(0,8,31)$ at $2.5$, scaled with $(a\sqrt{\sigma})^{-2}$): basis A, 21 operators (CP: the 7 shapes unsmeared). Vacuum-subtracted correlators averaged over all time slices, normalized by $\sqrt{\mathrm{diag}\,C(0)}$, ground vector from the generalized eigenvalue problem [25, 26] at $(t_0, t) = (0, 1)$, effective masses $m(t) = \ln C(t)/C(t+1)$. Plateau rule, fixed in advance: if $m(2)$ has relative error below 30% and $|m(1) - m(2)| < 1.5\sigma$ (jackknife of the difference), quote $m(1)$ ("plateau"); if $m(2)$ is that precise but differs, quote $m(2)$ ("late"); otherwise quote $m(1)$ as an "upper bound"; a quoted value with relative error above 30% is "noisy".

**String tension.** Estimator E1: $V(R) = \ln W(R,3)/W(R,4)$, fitted with $V_0 + \sigma R - e/R$, $e$ free, $R = 2,\dots,L/2$, with spatially smeared Wilson loops. Series 3 and the reanalyses of sec. 4.3 add E1$'$ (the same fit on $R \ge 4$), E2 (the same fit on $\ln W(R,T)/W(R,T+1)$ at $T = 5$ and $T = 6$) and Creutz ratios $\chi(R,R)$. Ratios $\sigma_X/\sigma_{\mathrm{ORIG}}$ use the same estimator on the same configurations. Literature values used for units and checks: $a\sqrt{\sigma} = 0.369$ ($\beta = 2.3$), $0.266$ ($2.4$), $0.188$ ($2.5$) [9, Table 10; 29, Table 7].

**Errors.** Delete-one-block jackknife, block length at least twice the largest integrated autocorrelation time of the plaquette and the $2\times2$ operator (Madras-Sokal window [27], $c = 6$), 8 to 20 blocks per stream; the bin-size scan of the VR mass errors (4 to 40 blocks per stream) is in data/bin_size_scan.csv. Autocorrelation times of the gauge-fixed observables themselves were not estimated. Thermalization check: first quarter dropped if its plaquette differs from the rest by more than $3\sigma$ (one stream in series 1).

**Pre-registered tests.** Premise C0: $\sigma_{\mathrm{VR}}/\sigma_{\mathrm{ORIG}} < 0.15$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{CP}}/\sigma_{\mathrm{ORIG}} \in [0.6, 1.4]$ with E1. Volume test at each coupling: $\rho = m(L_{\mathrm{large}})/m(L_{\mathrm{small}})$ in lattice units; "gap" if $\rho$ is within $2\sigma$ of 1 and more than $2\sigma$ from the box ratio $L_{\mathrm{small}}/L_{\mathrm{large}}$, "gapless" the reverse, otherwise inconclusive; interpreted only if the original-ensemble control reads "gap".

It is not zero at intermediate distances ($\chi(5,5) = 0.116(19)$, E1$' = 0.060(22)$). So the statement "the VR string tension is zero within errors" at $2.4$ holds for E1 and at long range, not at all distances; the $2.4$ and $2.5$ ensembles differ mainly in where the flattening falls relative to the E1 window. Item (b) of sec. 2 is this quantification.

# 5. Pre-registered outcomes

Each series fixed its combined rule in advance; we report them as written.

- Series 1: "gapless", triggered by $\beta = 2.3$ alone ($\rho_{\mathrm{VR}} = 0.733(111)$) while $\beta = 2.4$ was a "gap".
- Series 2: the combined rule as written reads "gap survives vortex removal (in this window)" ($\beta = 2.5$ gap, $2.4$ gap, no significant drop of $m_{\mathrm{VR}}/\sqrt\sigma$ from $2.4$ to $2.5$, $2.3$ not gapless). The rule did not say whether the series 1 consequence of a C0 failure (that coupling does not enter the verdict) carries over; with it, the outcome is inconclusive.
- Series 3 was designed to settle that by test. Its rule: if the best-of-16 VR E1 ratio is still $\ge 0.15$, the combined outcome is inconclusive, because the $\beta = 2.5$ VR point is not a clean vortex-free measurement under C0. The ratio is $0.225(10)$, so the **combined outcome is inconclusive**. The pre-registered gauge-fixing diagnosis (G) does not hold: with best-of-16 copies the residual does not fall (it rises slightly with the functional). Copies closer to the global maximum (simulated annealing, [14]) and Laplacian center gauge [2] were not tested. "Measurement artifact of the short-time estimator" and "real residual" are not separated by the pre-registered test, because the long-range estimators at $16^4$ have errors of about $0.2$.

# 6. Discussion

The cleanest point in this data set is $\beta = 2.4$. There the vortex-removed $0^{++}$ channel has a decay rate of about $0.7$ of the original glueball mass that does not change between $12^4$ and $16^4$: $\rho = 0.984(42)$, $5.6\sigma$ from the ratio $0.75$ that a level scaling as $1/L$ would give ($0.96(9)$ with $m(2)$). This volume ratio is the evidence against a finite-volume state, and it has two limits. First, the free two-gluon level we compare with, $4\sin(\pi/L)$, is the lowest non-zero-momentum level; in a periodic box the lowest free-field $0^{++}$ levels lie in the zero-momentum (torus) sector, with energies of order $g^{2/3}/L$ at weak coupling [18, 19], which can be below it. Being below $4\sin(\pi/L)$ therefore does not by itself exclude a finite-volume state, and we keep that comparison as secondary only. Second, with $\sigma_{\mathrm{VR}} = 0$ at long range a pair of Polyakov loops (flux winding the box) has no energy cost linear in $L$, so a volume-independent level could also be such a finite-volume state; we did not measure Polyakov loops. The $\beta = 2.4$ point rests on two couplings for any comparison, one gauge copy (with an untested copy systematic that was about 20% of the mass at the neighbouring couplings), and a string tension that is zero within errors with E1 and at the largest distances, but not at intermediate distances (sec. 4.3). Its mass is $2.40(8)\sqrt{\sigma}$ in our own unit and $2.52(8)\sqrt{\sigma}$ in the literature unit; the ratio $0.71(4)$ to the original mass does not depend on the unit.

At $\beta = 2.5$ the same picture holds for the mass, and the long-time estimators are consistent with [1, 2] at the largest distances we can resolve ($R \approx 7a$, about $1.3$ to $1.5/\sqrt{\sigma}$), but the pre-registered short-time check does not pass, and the VR mass moves at the $2\sigma$ level with the gauge copy. The center-projected string tension depends strongly on the gauge copy ($0.99$ of the full one with a single copy, $0.73$ with the best of 16, falling almost monotonically with the functional, as in [14, 15]); since the VR links are the original links with the center part divided out, the VR ensemble itself depends on the copy, and a VR mass should be quoted with that systematic. At $\beta = 2.3$ the VR correlator is not a clean exponential at the available times.

What would settle it: a premise check built on long-time or Creutz-ratio estimators fixed before new data, larger $T$ and finer couplings, a third box per coupling, Polyakov-loop measurements on the VR ensembles, and a systematic gauge-copy study of the VR mass at every coupling (including simulated annealing and Laplacian center gauge).
